---

Most DTC video editors cut when nothing's happening.

That's why your ads bleed reach.

---

I spent a week reviewing 40 ad edits from 12 different agencies.

The most common mistake wasn't the wrong music.

It wasn't bad captions or a weak hook.

It was the timing between visual events.

---

The human eye expects a new stimulus every 1 to 1.5 seconds.

Not a new scene. A new point of visual interest.

A caption animation. A zoom punch. A sound spike. A color cut. A text reveal.

---

When an edit gives the eye nothing for 3 seconds, the brain has time to wander.

That wander is a scroll.

Most DTC ads have 3 to 5 second gaps between visual events.

That's why they hemorrhage viewers between the hook and the CTA.

---

This is what I call the heartbeat method.

The principle: give the eye a reason to stay every 1 to 1.5 seconds, even within a static shot.

You don't need fast cutting. You need rhythm.

A slow-paced ad can still use the heartbeat method through:
- Timed caption animations
- Word-by-word text reveals
- Micro-zooms on product or face
- Audio beats that sync to cuts
- Background color shifts between sections

---

I reviewed the top 10 DTC ads from my feed this week.

All 10 had visual events on a 1 to 1.5 second rhythm in the first 6 seconds.

Every single one.

It's not coincidence.

---

The edit starts before the edit starts.

The rhythm is planned before a single clip is placed on the timeline.

That's the craft part. Not the software. The structure.
