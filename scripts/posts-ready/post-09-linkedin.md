Most DTC brands brief their editors with:
- A voice memo
- A Notion paragraph
- "Make it feel premium"

Then they wonder why revisions take longer than the edit.

---

Our internal brief template covers 5 categories.

I'm not going to explain any of them in detail here.

That would defeat the point of sending it to you.

But I'll name them:

**Hook direction** — not the hook itself. The emotional register the editor enters before frame one.

**Visual tone reference** — not "make it premium." An actual reference set with specific reasoning for each pick.

**Claim hierarchy** — which claim lands first, which supports it, and which ones never make it into the ad because they kill the pace.

**Platform context** — Meta feed, Reels, and TikTok require fundamentally different pacing decisions. One brief covers all three without rewriting it.

**Audience signal** — who this ad is NOT for. Most briefs skip this. It's the most important creative decision in the document.

---

The brief doesn't tell the editor what to do.

It eliminates every decision they'd otherwise have to make on their own.

Faster edits. More consistent output. Lower CPA — because the creative is intentional from second zero.
