# LinkedIn Content Playbook for DTC / Meta Ads / AI-Ad Creators: Who Leads, What They Post, and How FASTECH.PAK Should Reposition

The creators who win in this niche don't post "we edit ads." They sell a point of view, a signature method, or a system: Barry Hott's "Make Ugly Ads," Charley Tichenor's "3:2:2 Method," Sarah Levinger's psychology-based "CIM process," Adcrate's "creative engine." FASTECH.PAK's move from "we edit your ads" to "we give you a system for ad content production" matches what the top performers do. But the three pillars in the original plan are too narrow. The leaders rotate through about 10 recurring formats, and the most important of these are platform news and commentary, contrarian takes, ad teardowns, and build-in-public posts.

## TL;DR
- **Who to model:** Barry Hott, Dara Denney, Alex Cooper (Adcrate / Parker AI, UK), Ben Heath (Heath Media, UK), Savannah Sanchez (The Social Savannah), Charley Tichenor (Disrupter Academy), Sarah Levinger, and Nick Shackelford (Structured / Konstant Kreative). For AI-UGC specifically, add Romain Torres (Arcads) and Ciaran Finn (co-founder of the performance creative agency Linear). Each one owns a named idea or a signature format, not just a service category.
- **Tease-and-gate is common in the AI-UGC and agency-founder segment, and rare among the top educators.** Verified keyword gates include Alex Cooper's "AdScan," Romain Torres's "ai ugc farm," Ronak Shah's "Arcads" prompt spreadsheet, and Ciaran Finn's "ADCOPY," "GEMINI," "UGC," and "TEMPLATE." I found no keyword-gate posts from Barry Hott, Ben Heath, Sarah Levinger, or Savannah Sanchez. Dara Denney uses only a plain "comment and I'll send the link." Use it as one pillar, not the main strategy.
- **Positioning:** The top agencies (Adcrate, NewForm, Darkroom) use "engine / system / partner" language tied to testing and business metrics. Konstant Kreative is the counter-example, sticking with "design on demand" production language. FASTECH.PAK should adopt system language, back it with a named, documented method, and prove it through build-in-public and results posts.

### 3. How common is the tease-and-gate mechanic in this niche?

**It is moderately common, and concentrated in two groups:** (a) AI-UGC tool founders and their partners, and (b) creative-agency founders. It is largely absent from the highest-authority educators. Verified examples (quotes come from search-indexed LinkedIn/X snippets, so check the live posts before reusing them):

1. **Alex Cooper (Adcrate, UK)**
   - Hook: "If you're not doing competitor research for ads, you're wasting a chunky part of your ad spend." It teases a free Chrome extension that upgrades the Facebook Ad Library.
   - Gate: "Comment below 'AdScan' if you want access to this feature today - must be connected with me so I can send it."
   - Mechanics: keyword plus connection gate. On X he uses a variant: "RT + Comment 'Parker' and I'll send you 100+ AI prompt engineers for hire."
2. **Romain Torres (Arcads)**
   - Hook: "I am building a TikTok farm to distribute AI UGC: create 200 ai ugc with arcads.ai, distribute on 20 accounts over 5 days…"
   - Gate: "Comment ai ugc farm to get the guide."
   - On X he has also used "comment Arcads, and I'll share them in your DMs" (top 3 API workflows) and "Comment 'VOX' and I'll send you the skill" (a Claude skill for Vox-style ads).
3. **Ciaran Finn (Linear co-founder)** uses it as a serial format:
   - "6 Proven Ad Copy Templates for Winning Meta Ads" (226 comments): "Connect with me… and comment 'ADCOPY' below I'll send the templates over."
   - An AI-UGC post on Gemini Omni offering "the exact prompt library our team uses," gated with "Like this post - Connect with me Ciaran Finn - Comment 'GEMINI' And I'll send over the full document."
   - "25 UGC frameworks… for 8-9 figure DTC brands. Comment 'UGC' below."
   - A template library: "1. Connect with me Ciaran Finn 2. Comment 'TEMPLATE' below And I'll send you the full library straight over."
4. **Ronak Shah** (an Arcads partner) posted after Arcads announced a $16M seed round led by Eurazeo in December 2025: "I am giving away a spreadsheet of 100 of the best prompts to use in Arcads today. Comment Arcads and I'll send it you. And yes, I'm an Arcads partner."
   - The comments show the risk: pushback such as "If an AI does UGC content, it stops being 'user generated content.'"

**Counter-evidence:** I found no indexed keyword-gate posts from Barry Hott, Ben Heath, Sarah Levinger, or recent Savannah Sanchez posts. Dara Denney uses only a keyword-free "Leave a comment and I'll send you the link." These creators monetize through courses, communities, and newsletters instead. **What this means for FASTECH.PAK:** gating works best when the resource is a real working asset, such as a prompt library, template set, or tool, and it pairs well with an AI-workflow hook. Used on every post, it reads as low-status growth-hacking. Limit it to about 1 in 5 posts.

| **Sarah Levinger** | Method / transferable system | The "CIM process… a systematic framework your team can run independently" |
| **The Social Savannah** | Process-based, founder-led service | "We leverage weekly creative testing with a variety of different ad styles… This process helps avoid burnout in your ad accounts"; clients get to work directly with Savannah by design |
| **Konstant Kreative** | Service / on-demand production | "Make Winning Ads." "AI creates. Humans finish." "Our design on demand service creates ads you ask for," with strategy sold as an add-on |

**Conclusion:** At the top end, the market has moved to "engine / system / compounding learning" language. That language is credible only when paired with (a) a named, proprietary method, (b) metrics above creative level (MER, share of spend, hit rate), and (c) visible throughput and SLAs. Shackelford's own post sharpens the point: his value isn't that "humans are better or faster editors… than an AI model," but that "most people don't know what the hell they want or how to describe it." In other words, the system is the briefing, iteration, and feedback loop, not the editing.

## Recommendations

### A. Reposition FASTECH.PAK
- **Name the system.** FASTECH's version: "The Overnight Engine" — send the brief at night, get the finished ad by morning. Keep it simple, don't over-explain the timezone mechanic.
- **Sell outcomes and cadence, not edits.** Examples: "X concepts and Y hook variants per week, 48–72h turnaround, a weekly winners/losers report, and a creative-to-spend dashboard." Borrow Alex Cooper's metric: the share of account spend controlled by your creatives.
- **Turn the Karachi base into an asset.** Frame it as a follow-the-sun production pod (US/UK briefs in the evening, assets by morning) and document it in build-in-public posts.
- **Stay human-plus-AI.** NewForm and Konstant Kreative both stress that humans edit and finish the work. AI UGC draws visible ethics pushback, so position AI as a speed and volume layer and keep humans as the quality and strategy layer.

## Caveats
- LinkedIn blocks automated reading, so post quotes and follower counts come from search-engine snippets and cached profile pages that may be months or years old. For example, Savannah Sanchez's 17,863 followers comes from an undated snapshot. Verify on the live profiles before citing them externally.
- Some creators (Barry Hott, Dara Denney) have their largest audiences on X or YouTube. LinkedIn follower counts for several were not retrievable.
- Romain Torres is based in France, which falls outside a strict US/UK filter. He is included because he defines the AI-UGC tease-and-gate pattern, and Arcads says more than half its users are in the US. Ciaran Finn is US-based: his LinkedIn lists Miami Beach and his X profile lists Los Angeles. Ronak Shah openly discloses that he is an Arcads partner, so treat his post as affiliate content.
- Agency performance claims (Heath Media's "$300M+" ad spend, Barry's "$1 billion") are self-reported and unaudited. Heath Media has a 4-star TrustScore on Trustpilot from roughly 300 reviews, and the negative ones include "I asked for a refund after 1 month of very little help and was ghosted."
- Pillar percentages are a recommended starting mix inferred from observed patterns, not measured benchmarks. Adjust them after 6–8 weeks of your own analytics.
